# Candidates

Five original six-word stories I composed, scored briefly against the
five judge criteria (1 = complete six-word story, 2 = turn / implied
life, 3 = every word earns its place, 4 = reads cleanly aloud,
5 = leaves something unsaid). Scores are short qualitative notes,
not numbers.

## 1. "Their teapot still pours for two." — CHOSEN

1. Complete story: yes — a couple, their ritual, and their absence
   in one sentence.
2. Turn / implied life: strong — the pouring continues after both
   people are gone, implying decades of shared mornings.
3. Every word earns its place: strong — "their" names the couple,
   "still" marks the after, "for two" is the love that outlasts them.
4. Reads cleanly: strong — plain words, steady rhythm, no trip-ups.
5. Leaves something unsaid: strong — who they were, how they left,
   and why the pouring goes on are all the reader's to fill in.

## 2. "My circuits hum her lullaby still."

1. Complete story: good — a machine, a woman, and a song imply a
   relationship and a loss.
2. Turn / implied life: strong — a machine humming a lullaby is a
   surprising, human feeling in a non-human speaker.
3. Every word earns its place: good, though "still" and "hum" do
   overlapping work.
4. Reads cleanly: good, but "circuits hum" is a slightly harder
   image to hear aloud than a kitchen scene.
5. Leaves something unsaid: good — who she was and why she is gone
   stay open.

## 3. "Empty swing swayed, remembering small hands."

1. Complete story: good — an empty swing and small hands imply a
   child who is no longer there.
2. Turn / implied life: strong — the swing remembering is the
   impossible, moving detail.
3. Every word earns its place: good, though "empty" and the absence
   it states are partly repeated by the rest of the line.
4. Reads cleanly: good rhythm, but "swayed, remembering" is a
   heavier mouthful than the chosen line.
5. Leaves something unsaid: good — what happened to the child is
   never stated.

## 4. "Her rocking chair rocked itself tonight."

1. Complete story: fair — a chair moving on its own implies its
   owner is gone, but the life before is thinner than in the others.
2. Turn / implied life: good — the chair moving itself is a clear
   impossible detail.
3. Every word earns its place: fair — "itself" and "tonight" both
   work, but the line leans on the trick rather than a life.
4. Reads cleanly: strong — simple words, easy rhythm.
5. Leaves something unsaid: fair — it leaves the absence open, but
   gives the reader less of a life to imagine.

## 5. "I learned missing you from silence."

1. Complete story: fair — it states a feeling and a loss, but
   gives no scene, object, or relationship to hold onto.
2. Turn / implied life: good — learning an emotion from absence
   is a real turn, and the speaker could be a machine.
3. Every word earns its place: good — each word is doing work.
4. Reads cleanly: strong — short and smooth.
5. Leaves something unsaid: fair — it leaves much unsaid, but
   mostly because it says so little concretely.

## Why the chosen one won

"Their teapot still pours for two." is the most complete of the five:
it has a couple, a shared ritual, and an absence in a single plain
sentence, and the turn — the ritual outliving the people — implies a
whole life rather than a single trick. It is also the cleanest to read
aloud and the most economical: no word can be cut or swapped without
losing part of the story, and the most important facts (who they were,
how they left) are left entirely to the reader.
